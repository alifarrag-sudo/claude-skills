# Brief Failure Cases — Real Patterns

Six real-pattern brief failures showing the stated problem, the real problem, and what the strategy should have addressed. Use to calibrate when a brief is misleading and in which direction.

---

## Case 1 — The Awareness Brief That Was Actually a Trust Problem

**Category:** Healthcare / Private Hospital

**What the brief said:** "We need to increase awareness of our specialist surgical department among patients aged 35-60."

**What was actually happening:** Awareness was not the deficit. Qualitative listening revealed that the target audience had heard of the hospital — but associated it with general care, not surgical excellence. The real problem was a credibility and positioning gap: people didn't know to think of this hospital for serious conditions needing specialist surgery because nothing in the brand's communication signaled that depth of specialization.

**What the strategy should have addressed:** Not awareness — trust and clinical credibility. A campaign that increases awareness of a hospital people don't trust accelerates the wrong impression. The brief needed to be reframed as a credibility-building exercise first, with awareness as the downstream effect.

**The reframed brief in one sentence:** *Help qualified patients understand that this specialist surgical team is the reason to choose us — not just a department we have.*

## Case 2 — The Youth Audience Brief That Was Actually a Retention Problem

**Category:** F&B / QSR

**What the brief said:** "We need to attract younger audiences (18-24) to grow our customer base in the region."

**What was actually happening:** The brand's existing base — 25-40 year olds with families — was loyal, high-frequency, and high-spend. The 18-24 cohort was low-frequency, promotion-driven, and unlikely to become loyal without a fundamental product or experience shift. The brief was born from a boardroom conversation about "the future" that hadn't been tested against commercial reality.

**What was actually broken:** The brand was losing share among its existing core audience to a competitor who had improved their family experience. The brief for youth acquisition was masking a retention problem with an existing profitable segment.

**The reframed brief in one sentence:** *Before we earn new customers, we need to give our current best customers a reason to stay.*

## Case 3 — The Rebranding Brief That Was Actually an Internal Alignment Problem

**Category:** B2B Tech / Joint Venture

**What the brief said:** "We need a new brand identity and communication strategy that positions us as a leader in our category."

**What was actually happening:** The partner entities under the joint venture had not aligned on what the brand stood for internally. Each business unit had a different understanding of the value proposition, the target customer, and the competitive positioning. The rebranding brief was an attempt to solve externally what hadn't been solved internally.

**What the strategy should have addressed:** Internal brand architecture and alignment before external communication. A brand identity launched on top of internal misalignment produces incoherent communication — each team interprets the new brand through their existing (conflicting) lens.

**The reframed brief in one sentence:** *Build internal alignment on what this brand means before asking the market to believe it.*

## Case 4 — The Social Media Brief That Was Actually a Distribution Problem

**Category:** FMCG / Retail Launch

**What the brief said:** "Create a social media campaign to drive trial and awareness of our product in the new market."

**What was actually happening:** Distribution was incomplete. The product was in well under half the relevant retail outlets in the capital and not yet on shelf in the second city. A social media campaign driving awareness to an undistributed product creates demand that can't be fulfilled, which produces frustration rather than loyalty.

**What the strategy should have addressed:** The campaign timing needed to align with distribution reach, or the campaign needed to be redesigned around channels where availability could be guaranteed (e.g., e-commerce first).

**The reframed brief in one sentence:** *Don't create demand you can't fulfill — sequence the campaign to follow, not lead, distribution.*

## Case 5 — The Brand Love Brief That Was Actually a Product Brief

**Category:** Loyalty Program / Community

**What the brief said:** "We want to build brand love and community among our members."

**What was actually happening:** Member survey data showed that the primary reason people didn't engage with the program wasn't emotional — it was functional. The points redemption process was cumbersome, the app had friction, and the reward catalogue didn't match member aspirations. "Brand love" was being asked to compensate for product shortcomings that communication cannot fix.

**What the strategy should have addressed:** The product experience first, then the communication strategy. Brand love campaigns built on top of a frustrating product experience create ironic awareness — people become more aware of the gap between the promise and the reality.

**The reframed brief in one sentence:** *Fix the thing that makes members feel unseen before asking them to feel belonging.*

## Case 6 — The Ramadan Campaign Brief That Was Actually a Year-Round Relevance Problem

**Category:** Retail / Fashion

**What the brief said:** "Create a Ramadan campaign that drives engagement and sales during the season."

**What was actually happening:** The brand had no communication strategy outside of seasonal bursts (Ramadan, National Day, Eid). Members of the target audience had no relationship with the brand in the eleven months that weren't Ramadan — which meant the Ramadan campaign was being asked to do the work of an entire year's relationship-building in thirty days.

**What the strategy should have addressed:** A year-round communication framework that built genuine relationship, with Ramadan as the emotional peak of an ongoing conversation — not a cold start.

**The reframed brief in one sentence:** *A Ramadan campaign from a brand people don't know the rest of the year is just another ad — build the relationship first.*

---

## The Pattern Across All Six Cases

Every brief failure follows the same structure:
1. The stated problem is real but surface-level
2. The communications solution is being asked to compensate for something communications cannot fix
3. The real problem is upstream — product, distribution, internal alignment, sequencing, or relationship-building
4. Solving the stated problem without addressing the real problem produces confident, expensive misdirection

The interrogation exists to find the upstream problem before the strategy begins.
