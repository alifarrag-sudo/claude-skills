# Opening Lines — Strong Deck Openers

20 examples of strong deck-opening lines across different strategic contexts. These are not templates to copy — they are calibration examples. The quality bar: a line that makes a senior client stop scrolling and read it again.

---

## Healthcare

*"The internet gave everyone a diagnosis. It didn't give anyone permission to feel okay about it."*

*"Your patients have already googled it. The question is whether you're the brand they trust when they arrive."*

*"The country is building world-class hospitals. The gap isn't the facilities. It's the human conversation inside them."*

---

## F&B / FMCG

*"Every family has a dish that only one person knew how to make. And that person is gone."*

*"We eat together because it's the only time no one is looking at their phone. We eat alone because sometimes that's all we can manage."*

*"The best brands in this category aren't selling food. They're selling the right to stop and sit down."*

---

## Retail / Fashion

*"She knew who she was long before the world was ready to see it."*

*"The public wardrobe is finally catching up with the private one."*

*"In this market, the question was never 'will people spend?' — it was 'what are they spending toward?'"*

---

## Automotive

*"The car isn't what you drive. It's where you go to be yourself."*

*"In a city that's changing faster than anyone planned, the only private space left is moving at 120 kilometers per hour."*

*"They say they want performance. What they mean is: they want to feel like someone who deserves it."*

---

## B2B / Tech

*"The people who choose enterprise technology rarely get credit when it works. They get blamed when it doesn't."*

*"Digital transformation isn't a technology problem. It's a trust problem."*

*"Three business units. Two legacy systems. One team that has to make them talk to each other. That's Tuesday."*

---

## Loyalty / CRM

*"Most loyalty programs reward behavior. The best ones change it."*

*"They have 40,000 points. They've had them for two years. Spending them feels like admitting something is over."*

*"The most loyal customers aren't the ones who can't leave. They're the ones who could, and haven't."*

---

## New Business Pitches

*"We read your last three annual reports, your CEO's last six interviews, and spent two weeks in your stores. Here's what we found."*

*"You don't have a communications problem. You have a clarity problem. Everything else follows from that."*

*"This category is talking past its audience. It's not a creative issue — it's a listening issue."*

---

## Notes on What Makes These Work

**Specificity**: The strongest lines contain a specific detail (40,000 points / 120 kilometers per hour / two years) that earns the reader's trust. Vague opening lines feel like filler.

**Tension**: Every strong opener contains a friction — something that pushes against something else. No tension, no engagement.

**Voice**: They don't sound like PowerPoint. They sound like a person who has something to say. The sentence is slightly more interesting than a normal person would have written.

**Short**: None of these opening lines need a second sentence. The first slide carries one thought.
