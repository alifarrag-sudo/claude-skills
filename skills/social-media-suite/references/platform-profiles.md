# Platform Profiles

This is the canonical reference for every content decision. Every caption, script,
hook, and format follows the rules for its platform. Content is not platform-native
if it is only a shorter or longer version of the same sentence: a TikTok script and a
LinkedIn carousel are different products for different mindsets, not two exports of
one idea.

## GCC and MENA note (read once, applies throughout)

For Gulf and Egyptian brands, three things override the generic guidance below:
- **Language mix is deliberate, not automatic.** Some categories live in Arabic (FMCG,
  family, food, government-adjacent), some in English (B2B SaaS, fintech, premium
  auto), many in a code-switch. Decide the mix per platform from what the brand and
  its audience actually use, observed in listening, not from a default.
- **Arabic text is always real, editable text, never AI-rendered pixels.** When a
  caption or on-screen line is Arabic, write it as Arabic text and flag any
  Arabic-in-image asset for proper typesetting (Amiri or Tajawal, RTL shaping).
- **Local rhythm matters.** Posting times, weekend (Fri/Sat in the Gulf), prayer
  times, Ramadan, and national days shift what works and when. Factor the market
  calendar into timing and theme.

Contents: LinkedIn, X, Instagram Feed, Instagram Reels, Instagram Stories, Facebook,
TikTok, YouTube Shorts, YouTube Long-Form, Threads, Pinterest, Reddit.

## LinkedIn

**What it is:** Professional network, the primary B2B platform. High-intent audience
in a work mindset.

**Audience:** Decision-makers, practitioners, industry professionals, roughly 25 to 54.

**What performs:** bold data-led hooks (only the first two lines show before "see
more", so the first line is everything); native carousel documents (high saves and
shares); short video under 60 seconds with captions burned in; contrarian opinions;
founder and executive personal posts (far more reach than company pages);
behind-the-scenes and team culture.

**What does not:** generic promo ("We're excited to announce"), long unbroken
paragraphs, stock photography, external links in the post body (the algorithm
suppresses them, so links go in the first comment).

**Caption rules:**
- Static: three lines max before "see more". Line 1 hook, line 2 context, line 3 CTA
  or question.
- Carousel: two lines plus "Swipe" or similar.
- Video: one to two lines on what it is.
- Hashtags: two to three, at the very end, specific (#DevSecOps, not #Tech).
- URLs never in the body, always in the first comment.

**Best formats:** carousel (highest saves), static stat cards, short video, text-only
opinion.

**Posting:** three to five times a week, Tue to Thu, 8 to 10am and 12 to 2pm local.
In the Gulf, Sun to Thu is the work week; treat Sunday like a Monday.

## X (Twitter)

**What it is:** Real-time conversation. Organic reach is declining but the right
engagement drives outsized distribution.

**Audience:** journalists, developers, founders, investors, power users.

**What performs:** very short punchy takes (one to two sentences); teaching threads
(numbered, connected); contrarian opinions with receipts; genuine reactions to
breaking moments; quote-tweets with a strong take; polls; images with bold text
overlay where the image is the content.

**What does not:** long blocks in a single tweet, too many hashtags, automated
cross-posts from LinkedIn (wrong tone), formal brand-speak.

**Caption rules:**
- 280 character hard limit; aim under 200 to leave room for quote-tweeting.
- Hashtags 0 to 1, only if genuinely trending or campaign-specific.
- End on a question or provocation to drive replies.
- Threads: tweet 1 is a standalone hook, each later tweet is one idea.

**Best formats:** short text takes, threads, images with text, video clips (first
three seconds decide it).

**Posting:** one to three a day, weekdays 9am to 12pm and 5 to 7pm.

## Instagram Feed

**What it is:** Visual-first. Discovery via Explore and hashtags, community via
comments and DMs.

**Audience:** broad consumer and prosumer. B2B works if the visual quality is high.

**What performs:** high-quality visuals (consistency matters more than which style);
carousels (pushed for dwell time); behind-the-scenes; data visualisations as
carousels; relatable content; a strong first frame that earns the swipe.

**What does not:** low-quality images, too much text on a static, bare product shots
without context, inconsistent visual style.

**Caption rules:**
- Two lines visible before "more"; line 1 is the hook.
- Hashtags five to eight, on a new line below the caption or in the first comment.
- Mix niche, mid, and broad tags.
- Always fill alt text (accessibility and SEO).

**Best formats:** carousel (highest reach), strong statics, Reels.

**Posting:** four to seven a week for feed, daily for Stories.

## Instagram Reels

**What it is:** Short-form video with the platform's highest organic reach; served
aggressively to non-followers.

**What performs:** hook in the first one to three seconds (on-screen text, bold
statement, or surprising visual); trending audio; on-screen captions (most views are
sound-off); under 30 seconds for most brands; fast-value education ("3 things your
team doesn't know about X"); B-roll with voiceover is fine, no need for face-to-camera.

**What does not:** slow intros ("Hi guys", logo stings), repurposed horizontal video,
weak audio on voiceovers, no text overlay.

**Caption rules:** slightly longer is fine (two to three lines); hashtags three to
five; include a CTA ("Save this", "Share with your team").

**Best formats:** text-on-screen plus VO, B-roll montage with VO, talking head with
captions.

**Posting:** four to seven a week; evenings and weekends perform well.

## Instagram Stories

**What it is:** Ephemeral 24-hour content, shown only to followers. High intimacy,
low pressure.

**What performs:** interactive stickers (polls, questions, quizzes, sliders);
behind-the-scenes; countdown timers; reposts of feed content with a "new post"
sticker; link stickers; quick polls; Q&A.

**What does not:** static promo with no interactivity, obvious afterthoughts,
text-heavy slides.

**Content rules:**
- One idea per frame; up to about seven frames before drop-off.
- At least one interactive element per series.
- Link stickers on any story referencing external content.
- Three to five Stories a day, separate from feed, more spontaneous than feed.

**Spec:** 9:16 vertical, 1080x1920. Keep text in the safe zone (middle 60%; UI clips
top and bottom).

## Facebook

**What it is:** Largest platform by users. Organic reach for brand pages is very low
(1 to 5%), but strong for community (Groups), events, and paid amplification. In many
MENA markets Facebook still carries real reach, especially in Egypt.

**Audience:** skews older (35 to 65), strong in international and MENA markets, good
for B2C and local business.

**What performs:** native video; long-form storytelling posts (this audience reads
more than X's); events and local content; Groups engagement; shared articles with a
strong comment hook; native polls.

**What does not:** terse X-style posts, excessive external links, pure promo without
narrative.

**Caption rules:** three to five sentences is fine; conversational and a touch warmer
than LinkedIn; end on a question; hashtags one to two; emojis sparingly.

**Posting:** one to two a day, weekdays 1 to 4pm.

## TikTok

**What it is:** Short-form video with the most powerful discovery algorithm in
social. A single video can reach millions of non-followers. It rewards watch time,
completion, and saves, not follower count.

**Audience:** skews 18 to 34 and expanding to 35 to 54. B2B is growing here.

**What performs:** a hook in the first one to two seconds; native formats (POV, "I
tried X so you don't have to", "Things nobody tells you about X", "Day in the life of
a [role]"); trending and original audio; creator-style authenticity over polish;
Stitches and Duets; fast education ("3 facts in 30 seconds"); VO over text cards;
jump-cut editing.

**What does not:** horizontal video (never), slow intros, corporate-feel production,
repurposed LinkedIn video, long setup before the point.

**Caption rules:** one to two lines (the video is the content); three to five
hashtags (one broad, one niche, one trending); often ends on a question; strong CTA.

**Script rules (TikTok-specific, the definitive version):**
- On-screen text appears at second 0 (not 3, not 5).
- First spoken word at second 0; no silent intro.
- Hook patterns: "Nobody in [industry] talks about this, but...", "Here's what [X]
  actually means for [role]...", "POV: you're a [ICP role] and...".
- Length 15 to 45 seconds is the B2B sweet spot.
- Claude cannot pick trending audio: mark the scene where trending sound goes and
  leave the actual track to the editor at production time (it changes weekly).
- Flag Duet/Stitch candidates where a competitor or industry post fits.
- End on a comment-bait line ("Comment [word] if you want the full breakdown").

**Best formats:** text-on-screen plus VO, creator-style explainer, B-roll with VO,
reaction/commentary.

**Posting:** one to four a day (higher than other platforms); timing matters less
than the algorithm.

## YouTube Shorts

**What it is:** YouTube's vertical short-form (under 60 seconds), in the Shorts feed
and searchable. Stronger SEO than TikTok: Shorts can surface in Google Search.

**What performs:** educational explainers, "quick tip" format, repurposed long-form
highlights, step-by-step how-to, a strong hook in three seconds, visuals that loop
cleanly.

**What does not:** no clear payoff, slow intros, horizontal video, clips that only
make sense with context the viewer does not have.

**Content rules:** the title carries searchability (include the keyword people would
search); description first two lines show in feed (keyword plus CTA); unlike TikTok,
an SEO title beats a pure hook; link the parent long-form video in the description if
one exists.

**Script rules:** same structure as a video script but faster; first three seconds is
hook text plus VO together; land the payoff by the 45-second mark, leaving room for
the CTA.

**Posting:** two to five a week; consistency over volume.

## YouTube Long-Form

**What it is:** The world's second-largest search engine. Long shelf life, high trust
and intent, ranks in YouTube and Google.

**Audience:** research-mode, willing to invest 5 to 20 minutes. Strong for tutorials,
case studies, interviews, documentary-style content.

**What performs:** evergreen how-to, case studies and customer stories, expert
interviews and panels, thought-leadership series, product walkthroughs, industry
analysis.

**What does not:** no chapters, bad audio (the single biggest drop-off driver), weak
thumbnails, titles that miss search intent.

**Content spec per video:**
- Title: SEO-optimised, under 60 characters, primary keyword in. Pattern: "[What
  they'll learn] | [Context]".
- Thumbnail brief: what appears (person, text overlay, brand element), high contrast,
  single focal point.
- Description: lines 1 to 2 show in search (hook plus keyword); below the fold cover
  what's inside, chapters, links, CTA.
- Chapters: always timestamped (0:00 Intro, 1:30 Topic 1, ...).
- Tags: five to ten keyword tags.
- End screen: which CTA (subscribe, watch next, visit site).
- Cards: where mid-video cards go and what they point to.

**Posting:** one to two a week, consistency over volume.

## Threads

**What it is:** Meta's text platform. The most brand-safe X alternative: casual,
conversational, discovery-oriented, cross-posts easily from Instagram.

**What performs:** opinions and hot takes (looser than LinkedIn), conversation-sparking
questions, behind-the-scenes, short threads (three to five posts), reposts with
commentary, genuine humour.

**What does not:** press-release tone, heavy promo, over-posting.

**Caption rules:** conversational first-person; no established hashtag culture, so
skip them for now; two to four lines for a single post; in threads, one idea per
post, under three lines each.

**Posting:** two to four a week; building slowly is fine.

## Pinterest

**What it is:** A visual search engine, not a social network. Users are in a
high-intent, aspirational, planning mindset. SEO matters far more than social
strategy here.

**Audience:** skews female, 25 to 45. Strong for lifestyle, home, food, fashion,
wedding, travel; growing B2B for infographics and design inspiration.

**What performs:** vertical images (2:3, 1000x1500 ideal), infographics and data viz,
step-by-step process content, listicles as visuals, lifestyle-context product
imagery, bold text overlay on a minimal background.

**What does not:** horizontal images, images without text overlay, missing
keyword-rich descriptions, pinning with no board strategy.

**Content spec:**
- Pin title: 60 to 100 characters, primary keyword natural.
- Pin description: 100 to 300 characters, keyword-rich, what it is and who it's for.
- Board: which board (each board is a topic).
- Link: every pin links somewhere (post, product, landing page).

**Posting:** five to fifteen pins a day (Pinterest rewards volume); use scheduling.

## Reddit

**What it is:** Community discussion with near-zero tolerance for promo. The
highest-trust engagement channel available to brands, if done right.

**Audience:** highly specific per subreddit, technically literate, will call out
inauthenticity instantly.

**What performs:** genuine value-first contributions to the conversation; original
data and research; honest practitioner questions; credible AMAs; sharing useful
resources with no ask attached; expert comments on existing threads.

**What does not:** anything that reads as marketing copy, prominent brand mentions,
self-promo without value, simultaneous cross-posting across subreddits.

**Content rules:**
- Reddit is a community engagement channel, not a publishing channel. Never post
  promotional content as a top-level post.
- Brand mention only if directly relevant and only in comments, never in a title.
- Write as a practitioner who happens to work at the brand, not as a marketer.
- One post belongs to one specific subreddit.
- AMAs: plan ahead, get mod approval, prep 10 to 15 pre-answered questions.
