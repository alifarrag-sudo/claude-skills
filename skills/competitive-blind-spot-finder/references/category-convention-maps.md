# Category Convention Maps — GCC

Pre-mapped conventions for common client categories, mapped for Gulf (GCC) markets as a worked set. Use to accelerate Step 1 of the competitive audit. These are starting maps — always verify against live competitive landscape before using in client work. If you work in other markets ({{MARKETS}}), use the same five headings to build your own map for each category.

---

## Healthcare (Private Hospitals / Specialists)

**Visual Conventions:**
White coats, clinical environments, stock-photo smiling patients and families, blue and white color palette, before/after imagery for cosmetic or surgical specialties, aerial views of hospital facilities.

**Verbal Conventions:**
"World-class care," "Your health, our priority," "Trusted by [X] patients," "State-of-the-art technology," "Compassionate care," "JCI accredited," "Your family deserves the best."

**Audience Convention:**
The healthy aspirational family (positioning around wellness rather than illness), the anxious parent (pediatrics), the aging patriarch or matriarch (chronic conditions). The actual patient — sick, scared, already diagnosed — is almost never shown.

**Emotional Convention:**
Reassurance, trust, warmth. The emotional register of healthcare advertising is almost universally: "everything will be okay." The anxiety, the midnight googling, the second opinion seeking, the fear — all invisible.

**The Biggest Blind Spot:**
The moment of diagnosis and its aftermath. What happens in the 72 hours after someone receives serious news? No hospital brand lives there — they all communicate from the position of the healthy person considering their options, not the person who has just received a result that changed everything.

## F&B / QSR

**Visual Conventions:**
Overhead food shots, warm lighting, steam rising, hands reaching toward food, families laughing around tables, young friends sharing. In GCC specifically: Ramadan lantern aesthetics, National Day green, iftar spreads.

**Verbal Conventions:**
"Made with love," "Fresh every day," "Bringing families together," "The taste you grew up with," "Because family deserves the best."

**Audience Convention:**
The happy family at the center of every meal, the young friend group, the nostalgic returning customer. The solo diner, the late-night hunger, the guilty pleasure, the meal eaten in the car — invisible.

**Emotional Convention:**
Joy, togetherness, warmth, nostalgia. No one in F&B advertising is impatient, hungry in a complicated way, eating alone and okay with it, or using food as comfort for something that isn't communal celebration.

**The Biggest Blind Spot:**
The private eating moment. Food is deeply personal — the midnight snack, the stress eating, the meal that's just for you. QSR brands especially have an opportunity to own solo indulgence in a way that doesn't feel shameful — but none of them have.

## Automotive (Saudi / GCC)

**Visual Conventions:**
Open highways (often shot internationally, not in the GCC), dramatic lighting at dusk, lone driver at the wheel looking purposeful, chrome and speed details, desert or mountain roads for SUVs.

**Verbal Conventions:**
"Drive your ambition," "Power and performance," "Innovation meets luxury," "The road is yours," "Engineered for you."

**Audience Convention:**
The aspirational young professional (always male, always alone), the family man choosing safety and space, the executive choosing status. The actual social reality of Saudi driving — the traffic, the social rituals, the car as gathering space — almost entirely absent.

**Emotional Convention:**
Freedom, power, aspiration, arrival. The emotions of the car as sanctuary, as private space, as thinking room, as the only place you're truly alone — untouched.

**The Biggest Blind Spot:**
The interior life of the car. Saudi Arabia's social and geographic reality means people spend significant time in their cars — often alone, often in traffic. No automotive brand has owned the car as private emotional space, as the room where you think, listen, and exist outside the demands of family and work.

## Retail / Fashion (GCC Women's)

**Visual Conventions:**
Studio shots with clean backgrounds, editorial-style product photography, increasingly: outdoor lifestyle shots in recognizable GCC locations, modest fashion signaled through layering and coverage, aspirational but not ostentatious.

**Verbal Conventions:**
"Express yourself," "Your style, your rules," "Celebrate her," "For every occasion," "Confident and beautiful." Post-2017 Saudi: "empowerment" language proliferating.

**Audience Convention:**
The modern Gulf woman — always aspirational, always confident, always already arrived. The woman who is still figuring out her style, the woman in transition, the woman who has two wardrobes (the one seen and the one private) — invisible.

**Emotional Convention:**
Confidence, empowerment, liberation. The emotions of private style, of dressing for yourself rather than for others, of the gap between the public and private wardrobe — unexplored.

**The Biggest Blind Spot:**
The private wardrobe and the negotiation between public and private self. Gulf women have long maintained a rich private fashion identity that their public dress code historically constrained. As that constraint loosens, the most interesting territory is the woman who has always known who she is — and is now deciding how much of it to make visible.

## Loyalty Programs (GCC)

**Visual Conventions:**
Points accumulating on screen, reward moments (gift cards, flights, hotel stays), celebration iconography, mobile app interfaces, smiling customers redeeming rewards.

**Verbal Conventions:**
"Earn more," "Redeem your way," "The more you spend, the more you save," "Exclusive rewards," "Because you deserve it."

**Audience Convention:**
The frequent spender who is actively optimizing their points. The vast majority of loyalty program members — who are passively enrolled, rarely redeem, and have a complicated, slightly guilty relationship with their unused points — invisible.

**Emotional Convention:**
Aspiration (the reward you're building toward), celebration (the moment of redemption). The psychological reality of loyalty programs — the mild guilt of unspent points, the sense that you're failing to use something you earned, the confusion about what you can actually get — entirely unaddressed.

**The Biggest Blind Spot:**
The gap between membership and engagement. Most loyalty program members are enrolled but not activated. No program has spoken honestly to the person who has 47,000 points and has never once redeemed them — the person who is loyal in behavior but disconnected in feeling.

## B2B Tech / Digital Services (Saudi / GCC)

**Visual Conventions:**
Abstract data visualizations, blue and white color palette, stock photography of diverse teams in modern offices, diagrams of interconnected systems, the CEO at a whiteboard.

**Verbal Conventions:**
"Transforming your business," "Digital-first solutions," "Built for the future," "Powering Vision 2030," "Your trusted partner."

**Audience Convention:**
The C-suite visionary who is strategically committed to transformation. The actual decision-maker — often a middle manager under pressure, navigating internal politics, trying to make a defensible choice — invisible.

**Emotional Convention:**
Confidence, partnership, progress. The anxiety of the technology buyer — the fear of choosing wrong, the personal risk of a failed implementation, the difficulty of justifying a choice upstairs — untouched.

**The Biggest Blind Spot:**
The person making the decision, not the vision. Enterprise technology buyers are not visionaries selecting between compelling futures. They are often cautious professionals managing significant personal and organizational risk. No B2B tech brand in the GCC has spoken to the emotional reality of that person — and it is the single largest driver of purchase behavior in the category.
