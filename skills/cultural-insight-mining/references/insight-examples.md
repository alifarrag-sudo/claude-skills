# Insight Examples — Strong vs. Weak

6 worked examples calibrating insight quality across different categories. Use to benchmark your own output before presenting.

---

## Category: Healthcare

### ❌ Weak Insight
*"People want to feel confident about their health decisions and trust their doctors."*

Why it fails: This is a category assumption. Every healthcare brand knows this. It tells you nothing actionable or surprising.

### ✓ Strong Insight
**INSIGHT NAME: The Informed Patient's Loneliness**

*The Truth: People today arrive at the doctor's appointment having already diagnosed themselves, researched alternatives, and read the side effects. They're not looking for information — they're looking for someone to confirm that they're not crazy for caring this much.*

*The Tension: The more informed they become, the more isolated they feel — because information doesn't give you permission, and only a human can.*

*Why Now: Health information has been democratized but health authority hasn't. There's a gap between what people know and what they're allowed to feel.*

*Brand Entry Point: A hospital that treats the patient as a partner in the diagnosis, not a recipient of it, occupies something no one else owns.*

## Category: Automotive

### ❌ Weak Insight
*"Saudi young men are proud of their cars and see them as an extension of their identity."*

Why it fails: Observation. Everyone in the category already knows this and acts on it. There's no tension — no "yes, but."

### ✓ Strong Insight
**INSIGHT NAME: The Moving Room**

*The Truth: In Saudi Arabia, the car is the only private space a young man reliably has. It's where he plays the music his family wouldn't understand, calls the person he hasn't told anyone about, and practices the version of himself he's still figuring out.*

*The Tension: He presents it as a status object. It functions as a sanctuary.*

*Why Now: As cities grow and homes get smaller and more shared, the private space of the car has become more emotionally loaded, not less.*

*Brand Entry Point: A fleet or automotive brand that acknowledges the car as personal space — not just performance — owns the emotional truth no competitor is touching.*

## Category: Food / FMCG

### ❌ Weak Insight
*"Families in the Gulf value sharing meals together and see food as central to their culture."*

Why it fails: This is a truism. It describes everyone in every culture. Nothing to build on.

### ✓ Strong Insight
**INSIGHT NAME: The Recipe That Doesn't Exist Anymore**

*The Truth: Every family has a dish that only one person knew how to make. And that person is gone. The recipe was never written down. It lived in someone's hands.*

*The Tension: They can make the dish — but it's never the same. And they know it. And nobody says anything.*

*Why Now: Three generations of families in the region are now living through a transition they didn't plan for: the grandmothers who carried the kitchen knowledge are aging. The knowledge is disappearing in real time.*

*Brand Entry Point: A food brand that positions itself as the keeper of inherited taste — the brand that helps you get as close as you can — owns something emotionally irreplaceable.*

## Category: Loyalty Programs

### ❌ Weak Insight
*"Customers want to feel rewarded for their loyalty and appreciated by brands they buy from regularly."*

Why it fails: This is what loyalty programs are supposed to do. It's the brief, not an insight.

### ✓ Strong Insight
**INSIGHT NAME: The Points Nobody Spends**

*The Truth: Most loyalty program members never redeem their points. Not because they forgot — but because spending them feels like admitting the accumulation is over. The points represent a future they're saving for but never quite arrive at.*

*The Tension: The program rewards them for loyalty, but they punish themselves by holding onto the reward.*

*Why Now: Post-pandemic, people's relationship with "saving for later" has shifted. The future feels closer and more uncertain simultaneously.*

*Brand Entry Point: A loyalty brand that gives people permission — even a nudge — to actually enjoy what they've earned, instead of collecting it.*

## Category: Women's Retail / Fashion (KSA context)

### ❌ Weak Insight
*"Saudi women are increasingly empowered and expressing themselves through fashion."*

Why it fails: Correct but late. This was the 2018 insight. Everyone has already built a campaign on this. It's now background knowledge, not a strategic edge.

### ✓ Strong Insight
**INSIGHT NAME: The Second Wardrobe**

*The Truth: Saudi women have always had two wardrobes — the one for outside, and the one for inside. What's changed is that the outside wardrobe is now catching up with the inside one. For the first time, the public self and the private self are allowed to match.*

*The Tension: The inside wardrobe knew who she was before the world was ready to see it. The permission came later than the desire.*

*Why Now: The most interesting fashion moment isn't women discovering expression — it's women finally making public something they've privately perfected for years.*

*Brand Entry Point: A brand that honors the long history of private style, and celebrates its arrival into public life, speaks with reverence instead of discovery.*

## Category: B2B / Tech

### ❌ Weak Insight
*"Business leaders want technology that helps them grow faster and operate more efficiently."*

Why it fails: Every B2B tech company says this. It's the category promise, not a human truth.

### ✓ Strong Insight
**INSIGHT NAME: The Decision They Can't Own**

*The Truth: The people who choose enterprise technology rarely get credit when it works. They get blamed when it doesn't. This creates a specific, unspoken preference: choose the safe vendor, not the best one.*

*The Tension: Innovation loses to CYA (cover your ass). The best solution is rarely chosen; the most defensible one is.*

*Why Now: With digital transformation under executive scrutiny, middle management is making high-visibility choices under low-visibility conditions. The stakes are personal, not just organizational.*

*Brand Entry Point: A tech brand that gives the decision-maker confidence — not just in the product, but in the choice itself — removes the hidden barrier nobody talks about.*

## Calibration Notes

**The "so what" test**: After writing an insight, ask "so what does a brand do with this?" If the answer is obvious and boring, the insight isn't specific enough. If the answer is exciting and slightly surprising, you're in the right territory.

**The "anyone could say this" test**: Replace the brand name and category in your insight. Does it still make sense for five different brands in five different categories? If yes, it's not an insight — it's a human truth too broad to be useful. Go deeper.

**The "first thing you think of" rule**: The first insight you generate is almost always the most expected one. It's the industry's insight. Write it down, then push past it. The second or third thing you find is usually where the real territory is.
