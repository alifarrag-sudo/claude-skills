# Leap Examples — Insight to Idea in Action

6 worked examples showing the move used, the insight it started from, and the idea it produced. Each example names the reframing move so the pattern becomes recognizable.

---

## Example 1 — Healthcare
**Category:** Private Hospital / Specialist Care
**Move Used:** Stolen Frame

**Insight:** *Patients arrive at the doctor's appointment already diagnosed from the internet. They're not looking for information — they're looking for someone to confirm they're not crazy for caring this much.*

**The Leap:** Steal the frame of a courtroom, not a clinic. In a courtroom, evidence is heard and judged. The patient has gathered their evidence. They need a judge who respects the case they've built.

**Idea Name:** The Informed Partnership

**The Idea:** We are the hospital that treats the patient as a colleague in the diagnosis. You've done your research. We've done ours. Let's compare notes.

**What It Produces:**
- Campaigns that show doctors and patients looking at the same screen together
- A pre-appointment tool that lets patients submit their research and questions before they arrive
- Messaging that acknowledges "you've probably already read about this" — and respects it
- Tone of voice that never talks down to the patient

**Why No One Else Can Own It:** The hospital's recognised clinical depth gives it the credibility to position as a peer, not a provider. A lesser hospital making this claim would feel arrogant. Here it feels like the natural expression of genuinely world-class expertise.

## Example 2 — Loyalty Program
**Category:** Retail Loyalty / Women's Community
**Move Used:** Elevation

**Insight:** *Women in close-knit social circles refer friends not because they want points — but because being the person who introduced something good to someone they love is itself the reward. The referral is an act of care, not commerce.*

**The Leap:** Elevate the referral from transaction to gift. The program isn't a points system — it's a way to give people you love access to something that made your life better.

**Idea Name:** The Gift That Keeps Introducing

**The Idea:** The program doesn't reward loyalty — it gives members something worth sharing. Every referral is a gift, not a transaction. The currency isn't points — it's the feeling of being the person who knew first.

**What It Produces:**
- Referral mechanics that feel like gift-giving (personalized invitations, not generic referral codes)
- Community storytelling around "the friend who introduced me to this"
- Micro-influencer activation built around genuine introduction stories
- A microsite that celebrates introducers, not just joiners

**Why No One Else Can Own It:** Most loyalty programs are built on extraction — spend more, get more. This program's community positioning allows it to build on *generosity* instead, which is a fundamentally different and much more ownable emotional territory.

## Example 3 — Automotive (B2B Fleet)
**Category:** Fleet / Commercial Vehicles
**Move Used:** Specificity Drop

**Insight:** *Fleet buyers don't choose the best vehicle — they choose the most defensible choice. The personal risk of a bad fleet decision is higher than the organizational risk, so safety (in the decision, not the vehicle) is the real purchase driver.*

**The Leap:** Don't sell the vehicle. Sell the confidence to choose it. The brand becomes the fleet decision that no one can question.

**Idea Name:** The Unchallengeable Choice

**The Idea:** We are the fleet brand that makes the decision-maker look right. Not just the vehicle that performs — the choice that, twelve months later, no one is asking questions about.

**What It Produces:**
- B2B messaging that speaks to the decision-maker's personal stakes, not just the fleet's operational needs
- Case study content framed around "why we chose X and what happened" (from the fleet manager's perspective)
- A decision support tool that gives buyers a documented rationale for their choice
- Sales material that explicitly addresses "how do I justify this upstairs"

**Why No One Else Can Own It:** Most fleet brands sell on total cost of ownership, reliability statistics, or service networks. None of them have spoken to the human truth of the person making the decision. This territory is empty.

## Example 4 — Food / FMCG
**Category:** Flour / Staple Ingredients
**Move Used:** Elevation + Stolen Frame

**Insight:** *Every family has a dish that only one person knew how to make. That person is gone. The recipe was never written down. People try to recreate it — and they know it's never quite the same.*

**The Leap:** Steal the frame of inheritance. Flour is not an ingredient — it is the medium through which culinary memory is passed. The brand becomes the keeper of what was almost lost.

**Idea Name:** The Keeper of Recipes

**The Idea:** The brand doesn't sell flour. It holds the ingredient that connects generations. The brand exists so that what your grandmother made can still be made — even imperfectly, even approximately, even lovingly.

**What It Produces:**
- A recipe archive campaign collecting family recipes before they disappear
- Packaging that references the idea of inherited taste
- Social content built around "the dish I'm still trying to get right"
- A partnership with food historians or grandmothers to document the region's culinary memory
- A Ramadan campaign around the dishes that mark the season in each family

**Why No One Else Can Own It:** Flour is a commodity. No brand in the category is playing in the emotional territory of culinary heritage. A leading regional flour brand entering a new market has a specific moment to claim this before anyone else does.

## Example 5 — Tech / B2B
**Category:** Locally Founded Tech Platform
**Move Used:** Category Refusal

**Insight:** *Local businesses don't lack technology solutions — they lack confidence that the solutions were built with their specific reality in mind. The fear isn't "will it work?" It's "was this designed for someone like us?"*

**The Leap:** Refuse the category convention of global-platform-meets-local-market. Instead, lead with local design as the primary value — not a feature, not a localization, but the founding principle.

**Idea Name:** Local Before Global

**The Idea:** The platform doesn't adapt global solutions for the local market. It builds local solutions that happen to work everywhere else too. The origin is local. The ambition is global. The order matters.

**What It Produces:**
- Brand narrative that leads with local problem-solving, not platform specs
- Case studies structured as "a local challenge, and what we built to solve it"
- Thought leadership content that positions the founding team as indigenous experts, not technology importers
- GTM messaging that explicitly refuses the "global platform, local team" framing

**Why No One Else Can Own It:** Every international tech player in the market claims to understand it. Local Before Global is a founding claim — it can only be made by something that actually started here.

## Example 6 — Media / Content Brand
**Category:** Creative Agency / Brand Building
**Move Used:** Tension Hold

**Insight:** *The best brands in the region are navigating a specific tension: they need to feel internationally credible and locally rooted simultaneously. Choosing one over the other costs them the other audience.*

**The Leap:** Hold the tension instead of resolving it. The brand's idea is that you don't have to choose — and the agency exists to help clients live in both places at once.

**Idea Name:** Fluent in Both

**The Idea:** The agency speaks the language of global brand-building and the language of local culture — not as translation, but as a first language in both. We don't help brands localize. We help them belong.

**What It Produces:**
- Agency positioning that explicitly names the tension clients face
- Case studies structured around dual-audience success, not just market penetration
- Credentials deck that demonstrates cultural depth and global standard simultaneously
- A distinct point of view on what "regionally native brand building" means

**Why No One Else Can Own It:** Most regional agencies claim local expertise. Most international networks claim global standard. The tension hold is the move that makes this agency genuinely different — it names the client's real dilemma and offers to live inside it with them.

## Calibration Notes

**The "surprised and then obvious" test**: After seeing the leap, could someone have predicted it from the insight? If yes, it wasn't a leap — it was a step. The best ideas produce a moment of "I couldn't have gotten there, but now that I'm here, I can't imagine being anywhere else."

**The execution test**: From the idea, can you immediately describe 5 different executions that feel related but not repetitive? If you can only think of one execution, the idea is probably actually a campaign, not an idea.

**The compression test**: Can you say the idea in one sentence without losing anything essential? If the idea requires three sentences to exist, it isn't unified enough yet.
