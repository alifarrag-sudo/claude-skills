# Dialect reference template (every dialect session fills this exact shape)

File: D:\Language\skill-build\references\<dialect>.md   (one file per dialect or sub-dialect you own)
Writing rules: no em-dashes; Arabic examples in Arabic script with a short English gloss; every rule cites 2+ independent sources (book, thesis, article, corpus evidence counts as one source when you cite video/snap IDs); tag your own inference [inference]; never invent a quote, word, source or page.

## 1. Identity
Where it is spoken (cities, tribes), sub-varieties, who speaks it today, prestige or stigma attached, how the rest of the country perceives it.

## 2. The 15 tells
The fastest markers that make a line read as THIS dialect, ranked by how strongly they signal it. For each: the marker, an example, and what dialect it gets confused with.

## 3. Sounds and how to spell them
ق ك ج ث ذ ض ظ realisations, vowels, affrication, feminine suffix, stress quirks. Then: how natives WRITE these online (spelling conventions seen in the corpus, with counts where you have them).

## 4. Grammar
Pronouns and suffixes; verbs (present prefix, progressive, future, past quirks, imperative); negation (all forms); demonstratives; question words; relatives; prepositions and particles; plurals and gender agreement; word order quirks; sub-regional splits.

## 5. Lexicon
200+ distinctive words and expressions in a table: word | meaning | example | sub-region | age (elder / millennial / Gen Z) | register | evidence (source or corpus IDs) | corpus frequency if counted.

## 6. Social phrases
Greetings and replies, welcomes, blessings, oaths, condolence and congratulation formulas, endearments, insults and their weight, terms of address by age and gender.

## 7. Generations
What elders say that the young do not, and the reverse. Gen Z markers, English and Arabizi mixing, gaming and social slang as used by THIS region's youth (corpus evidence).

## 8. Gender
Differences in women's and men's speech and writing where evidenced; feminine address forms.

## 9. Humour, memes and stereotypes
What this region jokes about, how its humour sounds, memes and catchphrases born here, the stereotypes others hold about it, and which jokes are off-limits for a brand.

## 10. Online writing
Spelling, elongation, laughter, emoji habits, hashtags, code-switching, as seen in YouTube and Snapchat comments and on X.

## 11. Copy rules
Do and don't list for writing ads, scripts and social posts in this dialect. Then 12 example pairs: a WRONG line (MSA leak, other-dialect leak, caricature, or wrong register) and the RIGHT line, each with a one-line reason.

## 12. Self-check
A checklist a writer or reviewer runs on a draft to confirm it reads as this dialect and nothing else.

## 13. Evidence index
Sources (from research\sources\<dialect>.md) and corpus items used (video IDs, snap IDs, watched sheets), with what each proved.
